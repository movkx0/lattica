GPU prover profiling and optimization

All fifteen aggregation pairs improved. Warmed median times fell by 4.24% for four inputs, 2.83% for eight inputs, and 4.59% for two concurrent eight-input jobs. The larger native-wallet follow-up was effectively unchanged.

This campaign profiles native wallet proving, four- and eight-input recursive aggregation, and two concurrent eight-input jobs. It compares the existing RNG-optimized prover with cheaper Goldilocks subtraction, a guarded Poseidon2 internal layer, and a separately evaluated witness schedule.

The final implementation is fused. Each workload below has five alternating pairs, unchanged proof parameters, fresh proof randomness, resource monitoring, and an independent CPU audit of every result. The baseline already includes the earlier random-stream batching improvement; these results measure the additional changes.

What the profiles showed

The warm four-input baseline recorded 44,650 OpenCL events with zero dropped events. LDE row hashing and transforms dominated the recorded device execution. The optimized warm profile recorded 24,533 events; its recorded execution union was 23.349 seconds, compared with 24.808 seconds for baseline. These are instrumented observations, separate from the uninstrumented paired timing series. Fewer launches also mean fewer profile records; final speedup claims use the separate uninstrumented series.

The baseline issued 21,415 witness dependency-level launches in the four-input trace. The selected implementation issued 1,304 corresponding launches. More than 95% of the baseline levels contained at most 128 operations, motivating the separate bounded fusion experiment. Full profiles also cover the eight-input worker and both workers in the concurrent cohort.

Host tracing exposed substantial public circuit construction, preprocessing setup, quotient compilation, opening work, and event completion time. Entered host spans measure elapsed time, and process CPU counters include all process threads and driver activity. Nested phases overlap and cannot be added as a CPU-work breakdown. Gaps between recorded GPU events include host work and unrecorded device commands; they are not a measurement of GPU idle time. Independent workers' device clocks were not merged.

The available Nsight Systems installation has no OpenCL trace option. This campaign therefore used native OpenCL queue/event metadata, kernel resource queries, host tracing, process counters, and NVML power telemetry. It makes no hardware occupancy or bandwidth-saturation claim.

Code changes and experiment decisions

  1. Goldilocks subtraction. Replace addition of a modular negation with subtraction and two bounded borrow corrections. The operation accepts arbitrary 64-bit lazy field representations, including noncanonical values, and remains equivalent modulo 2^64 − 2^32 + 1.
  2. Poseidon2 internal diagonal. Replace generic multiplications by the fixed width-eight diagonal with additions, subtractions, and modular halving. The host checks every exported diagonal coefficient before enabling this kernel variant. A different dependency-provided diagonal retains the generic path.
  3. Witness scheduling. The retained witness optimization batches up to 64 consecutive dependency levels with at most 128 operations each into one 128-thread workgroup. A global-memory barrier separates levels. Wider levels retain the existing kernel. Wave lengths use the reserved final word of existing instruction records, so the managed allocation plan and upload size do not grow. The measured default is limited to the V100 device selection; explicit reference and tuning selections remain available.

The arithmetic screen compared baseline, subtraction-only, diagonal-only, and combined kernels using independent integer oracles and interleaved GPU event timings. Individual hash shapes had mixed results for the isolated changes; the combined candidate was carried forward to complete proofs. Its three four-input screening pairs all improved, with a 2.93% median paired reduction. Fusion has its own five-pair comparison against the arithmetic candidate: median paired reduction 0.80%, range -0.99% to 1.58%, and 4/5 wins. Screening, warm-ups, diagnostic profiles, and controlled cache runs are excluded from final qualification medians. The machine-readable evidence retains their individual results and the final selection rationale.

Retain the combined arithmetic and bounded witness fusion candidate for final qualification. Arithmetic won all three initial pairs (2.93% median paired reduction). Fusion won four of five direct pairs with a small 0.80% median paired reduction and a -0.99% to +1.58% range; the unpaired medians are effectively neutral. The fusion result is treated as modest and variable, with a clear reduction in launch and instrumentation overhead. All 26 targeted checks passed and managed allocation peaks were unchanged. Final performance claims use the separate five-pair baseline/combined series across four workloads.

The earlier RNG batching source remains unchanged. The new changes preserve the verifier, protocol parameters, hiding, fresh random streams, and proof formats. Apple Metal settings were not modified; hardware qualification here covers the stated V100 OpenCL environment.

How GPU proving works

  • 1 · Admit and compile
    CPU resource admission, public AIR and witness dependency metadata.
  • 2 · Build and commit
    GPU public values, witness rows, hiding, transforms, Poseidon2 and Merkle trees.
  • 3 · Prove constraints
    GPU transcript, lookup argument, quotient evaluation and commitments.
  • 4 · Open and verify
    GPU openings, FRI, grinding and bounded readback; CPU serialization and independent verification.

The host controls a pipeline of exact finite-field operations. Recursive witness operations execute in dependency order. GPU transforms expand polynomial evaluations, and hashing commits them. The transcript samples challenges in the verifier's prescribed order. Lookup and AIR constraints become a quotient polynomial; the prover commits its chunks, evaluates openings, folds the FRI codewords, and gathers the requested authentication paths. Intermediate private matrices remain in their owning device session or explicitly admitted storage. Public compilation and preprocessing may be reused, while private buffers and randomness belong to one proving attempt.

Legacy wallet proofs use quadratic extension challenges and 96 FRI queries. Recursive proofs use cubic challenges and 128 queries. Both profiles keep hiding, a 16-fold LDE blowup, and 16 query grinding bits. A four-input tree has two pair wrappers and one merge; an eight-input tree has four wrappers and three merges. Aggregation starts with existing wallet proofs. The separate native JoinSplit benchmark measures creation of one fresh wallet proof from a fixed witness.
